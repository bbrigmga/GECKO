What is Grok PM?

A small program you run on your own computer. Once a month you click a button, walk away, and roughly an hour later you have a researched 15-investment portfolio for the coming month — the kind of work a human analyst team might take days to produce.

It is not

A trading bot. It does not buy or sell anything, does not connect to a brokerage, and does not touch your money. It only produces research and a suggested allocation.

It is

An automated research assistant. It gathers public data, asks an AI (Grok, from xAI) to read and reason over that data hundreds of times, and assembles the answers into one portfolio.

Why "monthly"

The output is a portfolio you would hold for one month, then re-run and rebalance. Markets move fast enough that a fresh read each month beats a stale annual plan.

Think of it as one analyst, working for about an hour, who reads ~500 company dossiers and the whole macro backdrop — then writes you a one-page recommendation. The difference is that the "reading" is done by an AI model, and the app handles the legwork of gathering every dossier.

The three words you actually need

You do not need to know what a "token" or a "prompt" is to follow this. But three words make the rest easier:

1. API (“ay-pee-eye”)

A door a service leaves open so programs can talk to it. The app uses three doors: one to Stock News (news headlines), one to Wikipedia (current events), and one to Grok (the AI). Each "knock" on a door is one request.

2. Prompt

A written instruction handed to the AI. The app has a few reusable instruction templates. Each time it fills one in with real data and hands the finished text to Grok. Grok reads it and writes a reply.

3. Token

Roughly a fragment of a word. The AI bills by the fragment — a few cents per thousand fragments. A long company dossier is a few thousand fragments; a short reply is a few hundred. The app tracks the running bill as it goes.

Analogy

Imagine a chef who can only cook from a written order slip. You write "here are 90 ingredients and 15 news clippings about this company — score it 1–100 and explain". The chef reads the slip, cooks up a one-page report, and sends it back. You do this 500 times with 500 different slips, then one final big slip: "here are my best 35 companies and the macro outlook — build me a 15-item menu." That is the whole app.

The app stitches these into one big "here is what is happening in the world" text block and saves it.

Step 2 — Write the macro report (1 AI call, ~1–2 min)

The app hands that text block to Grok and asks for a three-month US economic timeline plus a forecast table (interest rates, inflation, tariffs, jobs). Grok is also allowed to search the live web and X while it writes, so the report is not just a summary of the news — it can pull in fresh events.

This single report is saved and reused by every later step. It is the shared "macro backdrop" the AI keeps in mind when scoring and allocating.

Step 3 — Score every S&P 500 company (~500 AI calls, most of the run)

This is the bulk of the work. For each of roughly 500 companies, the app:

Why no macro in the score? The app wants each score to reflect the company itself — its financials, valuation, momentum, news — not whether the AI thinks a recession is coming. The macro view is applied later, once, at allocation. This keeps the ranking from being biased by one forecast.

Step 4 — Pick the finalists (no AI, instant)

The app sorts all scored companies by score, highest first. From that ranked list it builds two buckets:

Top 30

The 30 highest-scoring firms. Saved for display in the dashboard's firm-scores table.

Allocation candidates (~35)

A slightly wider, sector-diversified set — seeded with the best names under a per-sector cap of 5, then filled in so no single industry dominates. This is what Grok actually picks stocks from.

In compliance mode (optional), this step changes: only stocks above a market-cap floor (default $200B) are eligible, and the candidate list is a closed top-N. ETFs are still allowed but must be plain long-only funds — no inverse, leveraged, or volatility products.

Step 5 — Build the portfolio (1 AI call, ~1–2 min)

The app hands Grok two things at once: the full macro report from step 2, and the candidate company packets from step 4. Grok writes the final 15-row table with weight, instrument type, thesis, edge, and risk for each holding.

In compliance mode, the generated table is then validated against the rules. If any holding breaks a rule, the app makes one extra AI call asking Grok to rewrite the table to fix the listed violations.

What the AI actually does

Grok is asked three different kinds of question in one run. Each has its own instruction template:

The macro question

Asked once. "Here is a week of news and Wikipedia context — plus you may search the web — forecast the next 3 months of US economics."

Output: a multi-section report + a forecast table.

The firm question

Asked ~500 times, once per company. "Here are 90 financial fields and 15 headlines for X — score it 1–100 on stand-alone quality. Ignore the macro."

Output: a short report + a number.

The portfolio question

Asked once at the end. "Here is the macro report and my best ~35 firms — build a 15-asset portfolio to beat the S&P 500 for the next month."

Output: the 15-row table.

One firm reply, verbatim (shortened)

Investment Report: Recent news highlights intense pressure on semiconductor equipment stocks… Financials reflect trailing revenue at $29.02B, profit margins near 29.3%… Valuations show a trailing P/E of 51.59 and forward of 33.34… Economic outlook affecting the firm centers on July CPI and FOMC… Score: 52

The app reads that last line with a simple pattern match. The number drives the ranking; the text is saved and reused at allocation.

Dialing it down

Set Max Tickers to, say, 50 and the run becomes ~51 Grok calls + ~51 Stock News requests — a few minutes and well under a dollar. Good for trying things out.

Dialing it back up

A full 500-firm run is the real product. It is also resumable — if your laptop sleeps or the network drops, the next run skips every firm already scored and picks up where it left off.

Portfolio-only reruns are nearly free. Once a run has scored firms, you can regenerate just the portfolio with new settings in one Grok call (or two, if compliance correction is needed) — no re-scoring, no Stock News calls.

All ~500 company reports with scores — browsable in the Firm Scores table.

Each run is written to runs/YYYY-MM/YYYY-MM-DD_HHMMSS/run.json on your machine. The dashboard can load any past run, export the portfolio to a CSV, or print a PDF report. You can also star favorite runs to pin them to the top of the history list.

The whole thing in one breath

The app reads the world's news and an encyclopedia, writes a 3-month economic forecast, then reads ~90 financial numbers and ~15 headlines for each of ~500 companies, asks the AI to grade each one, picks the best ~35, and asks the AI one more time to fold all of that into a 15-row portfolio you can hold for a month. Roughly an hour, a few dollars, ~500 AI conversations, ~45,000 data points.

Glossary — if you want the words

API
A door a service leaves open so programs can talk to it. "Calling an API" = knocking on that door with a request.
Prompt
The written instruction handed to an AI, with real data filled in. The app has a few reusable templates.
Token
A fragment of a word. AI models bill by the fragment — a few cents per thousand.
Grok
The AI model from xAI. The app uses it via xAI directly or via OpenRouter.
S&P 500
The 500 largest US public companies by market value. The app's default universe to score.
Market cap
Total value of a company's shares. Used to sort the universe (largest first) and as the compliance floor.
Concurrency
How many AI calls happen at once. Default 8 — like 8 cashiers open at once instead of 1.
Checkpoint
A saved snapshot of progress. Lets an interrupted run resume without redoing finished work.
ETF
A fund that holds a basket of stocks or bonds and trades like one share. The portfolio can include these.
Compliance mode
Optional setting that restricts stocks to a large-cap list and ETFs to plain long-only funds.
SSE
Server-Sent Events — how the dashboard gets live progress updates as the run proceeds.
Rebalance
Adjusting the portfolio back to target weights. This app's output is meant to be held one month, then re-run.
